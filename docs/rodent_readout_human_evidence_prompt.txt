Task: find human evidence for target-symptom claims that the Open Targets target safety curation records only as rodent behavioural readouts.

Context. A study maps "pharmacological activation or inhibition of gene X causes symptom Y" claims onto 23 psychiatric and neurological symptoms. 58 of the candidate claims rest on rodent readouts (locomotor activity, catalepsy, stereotypy). Rodent locomotor activity is not a human symptom, so each claim needs human evidence or it is dropped. Your job is to search the literature for human evidence for each claim below and report what you find, claim by claim.

The claims, as (symptom, direction, target genes):
1. psychomotor_retardation, activation: ADORA2A, ADORA3, ADRA1B, CACNA1C, CCKAR, CHRM1, CNR1, GABRA1, GABRA2, GABRA3, GABRA4, GABRA5, GABRA6, GABRG1, GABRG2, GABRG3, HTR2C, KCNN1, KCNN2, KCNN3, KCNN4, OPRK1, SCN1B, SCN2B, SCN3B, SCN4B
2. psychomotor_retardation, inhibition: DRD1, DRD2, SLC6A2, TACR1
3. hyperactivity, inhibition: CHRM1, GRIN1, GRIN2A, GRIN2B, GRIN2C, GRIN2D, GRIN3A, GRIN3B, HRH1, HTR1A, SLC6A2, SLC6A3
4. hyperactivity, activation: OPRD1
5. compulsive_behavior, inhibition: GRIN1, GRIN2A, GRIN2B, GRIN2C, GRIN2D, GRIN3A, GRIN3B, SLC6A3
6. compulsive_behavior, activation: DRD2, HTR1A, HTR2A
7. catatonia, activation: CHRM4, CNR1
8. catatonia, inhibition: DRD1, DRD2

What counts as human evidence, strongest first:
(a) a controlled human study (randomised trial, crossover or challenge study) where a drug acting at the target by that direction produced the symptom, measured in people;
(b) a pharmacovigilance or cohort study reporting the symptom for drugs whose principal action is that target and direction;
(c) a review or clinical pharmacology source stating the effect in humans;
(d) human genetic evidence (loss-of-function or gain-of-function variants) producing the symptom, which is relevant but is not pharmacological, so mark it separately.
Case reports alone are weak: report them but mark them as weak.

Important constraints on your reasoning:
- Many of these targets have no selective human drug. Where the only human drugs hitting the target also hit several others, say so and say which other targets could account for the symptom. A nonselective drug is not evidence for the specific target.
- Receptor subunits are a known problem: GABA-A and NMDA subunit genes (GABRA1-6, GABRG1-3, GRIN1, GRIN2A-D, GRIN3A-B) and sodium channel beta subunits (SCN1B-4B) rarely have subunit-selective human drugs. Do not credit a subunit with evidence that only supports the receptor family. Say which subunits a human drug is actually selective for, if any.
- Direction matters. Evidence for inhibition does not support activation. If you find evidence for the opposite direction, report that as contradicting evidence.
- "Sedation" or "somnolence" is not the same as psychomotor retardation, and "akathisia" is not hyperactivity. If the human evidence names a different symptom from the one claimed, report the symptom the human data actually support instead.

Method: use the PubMed tools available to you (load them with ToolSearch, query "select:mcp__PubMed__search_articles,mcp__PubMed__get_article_metadata"); if they do not respond, use the plugin equivalents (mcp__plugin_bio-research_pubmed__*) or the Consensus search tool. Prefer primary human studies and clinical pharmacology reviews. You must cite PubMed and include each article's DOI, as those tools require.
