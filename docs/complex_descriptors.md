## Is an entity vector comparable with a protein descriptor?

A protein's descriptor is the projection of the annotations *predicted from its sequence*; an entity's is the projection of annotations that are *recorded*. The two are different quantities, so the comparability is measured rather than assumed. Over the reviewed proteome, correlation between the two projections of the same protein: median 0.649 per component (minimum 0.546, maximum 0.842), and 64 of 64 components above 0.5.

For the 747 entities with exactly one member gene, the entity vector should resemble that gene's protein descriptor; the median correlation across those entities is 0.818.

Reading: the entity vectors share the protein descriptors' directions and units, but they are the observed-annotation projection, so they belong in their own block unless that correlation is high enough to justify one shared mapping.

## Entities without a descriptor

154 entities get none: 154 have no member gene (small-molecule-only, polymer or unmapped entities) and 0 have member genes whose annotations are not in the reviewed table. They keep a zero block and a flag column, as a gene without protein descriptors does.
