# Calibrating the placeholder concentration against the measured one

Generated by `experiments/label_review/placeholder_concentration_calibration.py`.

Estimated on the **115 drugs** that have both a placeholder and a measured concentration, comparing each against the geometric centre of its measured therapeutic range, in log10.

| reading | value |
|---|---|
| median offset | **+0.331 log10**, so the placeholder overstates the concentration by **2.1x** |
| mean offset | +0.434 log10 |
| scatter about it | sd **1.14 log10**, quartiles -0.26 to +1.08 |
| drugs landing inside the measured range, before | 27% |
| drugs landing inside the measured range, after | 30% |
| median absolute log10 error against the range centre, before | 0.75 |
| median absolute log10 error against the range centre, after | 0.70 |

## What this says

**The offset is real and worth taking.** The placeholder sits 2.1 times above the centre of the measured range, a systematic bias that costs nothing to remove and that nobody would want left in.

**It does not make an imputed concentration a measured one.** The scatter about the offset is 1.14 log units, about two orders of magnitude of drug-to-drug disagreement, and no single offset touches it: correcting moves the drugs landing inside their own measured range from 27% to 30%, and the median error from 0.75 to 0.70 log units. The bias is what a calibration removes; the scatter is what it cannot.

**So the offset belongs with a flag, not instead of one.** An imputed occupancy carries roughly a ten-fold uncertainty in the concentration behind it, and how much of that reaches the occupancy depends on where the occupancy sits: `d(log theta) / d(log C) = 1 - theta`, which falls monotonically as the occupancy rises. A one-sd error of 1.14 log units moves an occupancy of 0.01 by x12.2 up and x13.7 down, one of 0.5 by x1.9 and x7.4, and one of 0.9 by x1.1 and x2.3. So a saturated occupancy absorbs the error and a near-zero one passes it through almost whole. That is what the "has a measured occupancy" stratum is for, and the calibration does not remove the need for it.

**Corrected 13 October 2026.** This paragraph read "an occupancy near saturation or near zero absorbs and one near Kd does not". Both of its last two clauses were wrong and in the same direction: the sensitivity is monotone in the occupancy, so an occupancy near zero absorbs least of all, and one near Kd absorbs half. The error matters because `has_measured_occupancy` is reported against this relation and the withdrawn item 5b rested on it; the sentence stated it as a U shape.

**This constant offset is withdrawn as the applied correction; item 5c(ii) is a slope and a bias.** What this document still establishes is that the placeholder is biased and that the bias is worth removing. What it got wrong is the form: a constant cannot reach the dependence on affinity that the placeholder builds in, and it leaves the best mechanism target of every imputed drug at one and the same occupancy. Both are measured in `docs/occupancy_coverage_and_shift_transfer.md`, which carries the registered calibration. The offset below is kept as the baseline that calibration is scored against and as the record of what was registered first.

**One limit on the transfer.** The offset is estimated on the drugs that have a measured concentration, which are the better-studied ones, and applied to those that do not. If the placeholder's bias differs between the two, the correction does not carry. That is the same extrapolation this project registered for the Open Targets agreement, and it is stated rather than tested, because nothing measures it.
