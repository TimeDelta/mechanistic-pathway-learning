# Occupancy by target role: measured at an off-target, calibrated placeholder at a mechanism target

Generated by `experiments/label_review/occupancy_by_target_role.py`.

The owner, 13 October 2026: "Ooooh. I think it's better to put the measured values back then and calibrate the other way." The rule priced here gives the two target roles different input rules, because the asymmetry table carried by item 5a, and the rule registered as item 5b, shows the imputation error is absorbed at one role and not at the other: an off-target enters only on a measured concentration, a mechanism target may take the calibrated placeholder.

**No drug leaves the study.** A withheld drug keeps its mechanism targets and so keeps its input and its labels; what it loses is the off-target part of that input. That is the distinction the owner drew on 13 October ("Don't REQUIRE occupancy though ... some drugs legitimately don't have it"): the requirement here is on an off-target entering, not on a drug entering.

**Half of this is built in and should not be read as a finding.** The placeholder pins the best-bound mechanism target at 0.9 occupancy, which the calibration moves to 0.808, so every drug has one mechanism target at exactly that value by construction and the rest follow from the affinity ratios. What the measured drugs contribute is the test of whether the assertion is roughly right: their median mechanism occupancy is 0.787 against the asserted 0.808, so it is. The spread is where the assertion is not right, and the lower quartile at 0.503 is a reminder that a drug's weaker mechanism targets sit well down the curve even when its best one saturates.

## 3. What withholding an off-target asserts in the other direction

A withheld off-target is not abstained from: it is set to absent, which is the claim that the drug does not engage that target. That claim is wrong whenever the drug does. So the cost of the prohibition is not only the 578 rows it drops but the occupancy those rows would have carried.

## 4. Does the asymmetry survive the new allocation

The asymmetry was measured on the drugs that have a measured concentration, where the median mechanism occupancy is 0.787. What matters for this rule is whether the drugs that do **not** have one look the same, because those are the drugs the placeholder is carried to. They do: the median imputed mechanism occupancy is 0.806 and a one-sd error moves it x1.2 up and x3.5 down, against x1.2 and x3.7 on the measured drugs. The off-target figure the prohibition is built on, x7.6 up and x13.0 down, stands unchanged, because it is a property of where a median off-target occupancy of 0.063 sits on the curve and not of which drugs were used to find it.
