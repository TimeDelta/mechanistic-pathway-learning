# Human evidence for the rodent-only target safety claims

Generated by `experiments/map_rodent_readout_human_evidence.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The Open Targets target safety curation supports 58 (symptom, direction, target) claims only through rodent
behavioural readouts, which `experiments/scope_open_targets_symptom_labels.py` matches with `locomotor|catalep|stereotyp`.
Rodent locomotor activity is not a symptom a patient reports, so each claim needed human evidence or it leaves the
label set. A sub-agent searched the human literature claim by claim; this document records what it was asked, what
it returned, the checks run on the returned references and where each surviving claim lands among the study's
symptoms.

## What the search returned

| Verdict | Claims |
| --- | --- |
| keep as human | 3 |
| keep but relabel | 11 |
| keep as rodent only | 3 |
| drop | 41 |

`drop` means no human pharmacological evidence and either no human drug selective enough to test the claim or a
selective human drug whose controlled data show the symptom does not occur. `keep as rodent only` means the symptom
has no human evidence but the mechanism has indirect human support (inverse-direction pharmacology, an obligatory
subunit or human genetics) worth keeping as a rodent-derived hypothesis rather than as a label.

## Kept as a rodent-derived hypothesis, not as a label

| Symptom | Direction | Target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| psychomotor_retardation | activation | ADORA2A | No human A2A agonist study has measured psychomotor slowing; regadenoson trials report only cardiovascular tolerability. The only direction-relevant human data are inverse: the selective A2A antagonist istradefylline reduces OFF time and increases dyskinesia in Parkinson's disease, which is consistent with A2A activation being motor-suppressive but is not evidence that activation produces the symptom in people. |
| psychomotor_retardation | activation | GABRG2 | Gamma2 is obligatory for the action of every benzodiazepine-site drug that impairs human psychomotor function, so the human evidence is permissive of gamma2 but cannot isolate it - and the alpha-subtype dissection (TPA023 non-impairing, zolpidem impairing) shows the impairment tracks the alpha subunit, not gamma2. Retained as a rodent-derived claim rather than credited with family-level human evidence. |
| hyperactivity | inhibition | GRIN1 | GluN1 is obligatory in every NMDA receptor, so channel blockers act through it but cannot be attributed to it selectively. The human data do not show hyperactivity: in a randomised trial low-dose esketamine DECREASED emergence agitation, and the GluN2B-selective antagonist was well tolerated without dissociation. Retained as a rodent-derived claim only. |

## What the mapping leaves open

- A relabel that lands back on the claimed symptom is a mapping decision, not a finding. The study's pattern for
  `psychomotor_retardation` matches `\bbradykine`, so antipsychotic bradykinesia counts as that symptom here even
  though the cited cohort separates drug-induced parkinsonism from depressive psychomotor slowing; the pattern for
  `compulsive_behavior` matches `stereotyp`, so human punding counts as that symptom. Either the patterns stand, and
  these two claims carry human evidence for the symptom as the study defines it, or the patterns are narrowed, and
  the curation rows that matched the same way have to be read again.
- The human name for SLC6A3 inhibition, "punding / stereotyped repetitive behaviour", also matches the rodent-readout filter `locomotor|catalep|stereotyp`, so a curation row phrased that way is set aside as an animal readout before it can become a label for compulsive_behavior. Reading it as a human symptom needs an exception to that filter, not only this evidence.
- Every surviving claim is target-level: it says a drug acting at this target by this direction produces the
  symptom in people, not that a particular drug in the study's evidence table does. They enter as target-level
  rows or not at all.
