## What requiring a measured occupancy costs

- drugs with a reading: **121** of 222; without: **101**
- kept positive drug pairs carried by drugs with a reading: **810** of 1076 (75.3%); the rest, **24.7%**, leaves with them

The missingness is not random. A drug needs a published therapeutic range and a fraction unbound, which newer drugs are least likely to have, so the drugs that leave skew modern and include a good deal of the pharmacology a psychiatric study is for:

  Lumateperone, acamprosate, agomelatine, amifampridine, amoxapine, articaine, aspirin, atogepant, benperidol, benztropine, betahistine, brexpiprazole, bromazepam, bromocriptine, bupropion, cabergoline, cannabidiol, cariprazine, cenobamate, cevimeline, chloroprocaine, clobazam, daridorexant, deprenyl, desvenlafaxine, dexmedetomidine, dihydrocodeine, disulfiram, dothiepin, droperidol, ...

## Why those 101 drugs have no occupancy, which is not about their pharmacology

The owner, 13 October 2026: "Don't REQUIRE occupancy though ... some drugs legitimately don't have it, right?" and
then "All drugs should have that unless they are known 1:1".

Measured, and the gap is **entirely an exposure-data gap**:

| why a drug has no occupancy reading | drugs |
| --- | --- |
| no therapeutic range or concentration | 42 |
| neither a concentration nor a fraction unbound | 59 |
| no affinity (the other input occupancy needs) | **0** |

Every one of the 101 has an affinity at its targets. What none of them has is the pharmacokinetics: a published
therapeutic range, and for 59 of them a fraction unbound as well. So the missingness says how well studied a drug
is, not anything about how it binds.

**It is also not the single-target case.** If "known 1:1" means a drug with one target, that does not describe the
gap: 28.7 percent of the drugs without a reading have a single target against 29.8 percent of those with one —
the same population. If it means a covalent or otherwise irreversible binder, where an equilibrium occupancy is
the wrong model rather than a missing number, that is a real exception and three of the uncovered drugs are
exactly it — aspirin, selegiline and disulfiram — but three drugs do not explain 101.

**What follows for the design.** Occupancy should be computed wherever its inputs exist and not required, since
requiring it drops 101 drugs and 24.7 percent of the kept positive drug pairs for a reason that is about the
literature rather than about the drugs. The cost of not requiring it is the input-scale difference between the two
groups (median total input 1.00 against 3.0), and that is a confound to **declare and measure** rather than one to
avoid by deletion: item 4 already registers the input-size baseline and the input-size strata, which read exactly
this. The honest form is a mixed input with "has a measured occupancy" reported as a stratum, so the difference is
visible in every reading instead of being hidden in the drug set.
