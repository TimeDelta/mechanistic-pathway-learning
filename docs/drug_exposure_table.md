## Fraction unbound: the two sources compared

53 drugs have a fraction unbound in Lombardo's table and a percentage bound in their label (`configs/drug_label_protein_binding.csv`, each with its sentence and label version). The two differ by a median factor of 1.00; by less than 1.5 for 46 drugs, by 2 or more for 0 and by 3 or more for 0. The largest differences: clozapine (0.03 from the label, 0.055 in the table), valproate (0.142 from the label, 0.08 in the table), midazolam (0.03 from the label, 0.017 in the table), methadone (0.125 from the label, 0.21 in the table), lorazepam (0.15 from the label, 0.09 in the table).

Of the 38 drugs whose fraction unbound comes from the label, it is a value for 32, an upper limit for 4 (the label says "greater than") and a lower limit for 2; 2 are bound 99 percent or more, where the rounding of a label moves the fraction unbound by a factor of two or more.

## Hepatic extraction ratio

E = CL × (1 − fraction excreted unchanged in urine) / (blood-to-plasma ratio) / Q_H, with Q_H = 20.7 mL/min/kg.
Low is below 0.3 and high above 0.7, the usual cut-offs.

## What the numbers assume

- Clearance that is not renal excretion of unchanged drug is taken as hepatic. A drug cleared by plasma esterases or in other tissues gets too high a value.
- Clearance is of plasma after an intravenous dose; it is divided by the blood-to-plasma ratio to give a clearance of blood, and by 1 where no ratio was found.
- The therapeutic concentration is a total plasma or serum concentration at steady state as compiled by Schulz et al.; the range is the one the table prints outside brackets.
- The Kp,uu,brain is mostly from rats and mice. A human value is used where one exists. Values from knockout animals, from brain regions and from cerebrospinal fluid are left out.
- The unbound brain concentration assumes the steady-state ratio holds at the therapeutic concentration. It says nothing about the time course after a dose.

## Not retrieved

The consensus guideline for therapeutic drug monitoring in psychiatry (Hiemke et al. 2018, [doi:10.1055/s-0043-116492](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0043-116492)) gives therapeutic reference ranges for the psychotropic drugs, and would be the better source for them. It was not read: the publisher's robots file disallows automated readers, the article is not in PubMed Central, and no open licence was found (`docs/exposure_sources/hiemke2018_agnp.md` lists the 22 routes tried). The table of Schulz et al. already cites the guideline for many of its ranges. For the owner to open: <https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0043-116492.pdf>, and the repository copy that one index labels CC BY, <https://resolver.sub.uni-goettingen.de/purl?gro-2/74377>.
