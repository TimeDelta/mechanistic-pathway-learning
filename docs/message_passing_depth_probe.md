# Message passing with its layers reused for more rounds: probes with nothing trained

Generated by `experiments/probe_message_passing_depth.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Graph `data/processed/graph_full_neuronal_split_binders` (49,574 nodes), perturbations of `data/processed/evidence_full_v3_parkinsonism`: 8 gene knockouts and 8 drugs seeded on their targets, drawn with seed 0. No label is read and no parameter is fitted. The reach of a perturbation by number of hops, over every perturbation, is in `docs/drug_entry_nodes_measured.md`, section 4.

## 1. One layer's weights reused for every round

Read the norm columns down each block: if the size of the field changes by orders of magnitude with the number of rounds, a reused layer with the registered update is not stable on its own, and if perturbations stopped at different rounds the size of the field would carry the stop round.

## 2. What a round costs

One forward and backward pass of the registered encoder (own weights per layer), batch 16, each in its own process. Shared weights cost the same per round, because a round stores the same activations whichever weights it reads.

| layers | forward, s | forward and backward, s | peak memory, GB | memory after loading the data, GB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 1.2 | 4.4 | 1.47 | 0.79 |
| 6 | 2.2 | 8.6 | 2.04 | 0.79 |

Per added round: 1.4 s and 0.19 GB. Extrapolated linearly, not measured: 13 s and 2.6 GB at 9 rounds, 25 s and 4.3 GB at 18 rounds, 37 s and 5.9 GB at 26 rounds. The times were taken on two shared cores with other jobs running, so they compare rounds with each other and are not a benchmark. Seed masking propagates a reference beside every perturbation and doubles both.
