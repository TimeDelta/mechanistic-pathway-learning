# Decision 4.2: a complex taking its limiting member's cell-class weight

Generated by `experiments/label_review/complex_limiting_member_weight.py`.

## The question, and the answer

The owner, 13 October 2026: "Taking the complex's limiting member weight per type makes sense i think but only if it applies at the complex itself. That's what you mean, right?"

**Yes, and it is the only placement with the right semantics.** A complex is a `protein_entity` node that its members reach by `member_of` and that reaches reactions by `catalyzed_by`, `activates`, `inhibits` and `substrate_of`, so a response travels gene -> complex -> reaction and crosses the complex node. The encoder scales a response by the destination's class weight at each step, so a weight on the complex gates every path through it exactly once. The two alternatives are both wrong:

- **On the member genes alone**, which is what happened before this decision, gates each path by whichever   member it arrived through. An abundant subunit then lets the response past while an obligate one is   missing: a maximum over the members where a complex is a minimum.
- **On the downstream reaction** puts the weight where the complex is not, double-gates any path that also   crosses the complex node, and collides with the Human-GEM gene rule that already sets a reaction's   weight.

## What the minimum is taken over, which is not what was first assumed

A complex is the **"and"** of its subunits, and `evaluate_gene_rule` already reads "and" as a minimum over **raw** nCPM, with the relative weight formed afterwards. So the complex follows the same order: minimum on the raw expression, then `x_k / (max_j x_j + 1)`. The first version of this measurement took the minimum over the members' **relative weights** instead, which is a different quantity and a worse one: the relative weight is a specificity measure, so two subunits with different top classes pull each other down in every class and the complex ends up shut everywhere.

| over the complexes that can carry a gate | registered: min of raw, then relative | rejected: min of relative weights |
|---|---|---|
| median gate | **0.1500** | 0.0767 |
| quartiles | 0.0177 to 0.4951 | 0.0064 to 0.3620 |
| below 0.1, largely shut in that class | 44.0% | 52.8% |
| at or above 0.5, barely gated | 24.7% | 17.5% |
| **complexes available in at least one class at 0.9 or above** | **68.9%** | 45.4% |

The last row is the one that decides it. A complex that reaches 0.9 in no class is a complex nothing propagates through anywhere, and the rejected order leaves 55% of them in that state against 31% under the registered order.

## The reactions it reaches, and a correction

`docs/open_decisions.md` recorded **413** reactions regulated only by a complex. That number does not reproduce under any reading of "regulated", and is corrected here to the three that do:

| what counts as regulating the reaction | reactions whose regulators are all complexes | of those, now gated |
|---|---|---|
| catalyzed_by alone | 343 | **338** |
| catalysis and regulation | 387 | **382** |

The middle row is the reading this decision is about, since `substrate_of` is a complex entering a reaction as a reactant rather than regulating it.

## Why the minimum has to be taken per class

Over the 1523 complexes with a computable gate, the limiting member is the same in every class for 968 and differs across classes for **555**, up to 6 distinct limiting subunits. Choosing one limiting subunit globally would therefore be a different rule and a weaker one, carrying the wrong subunit's profile in every class where another is scarcer. The minimum is taken within each class, which is what "per type" asks for.

## Two consequences, one of them a problem

**The compounding along a path is the encoder's standing convention, not a new double count.** A response on gene -> complex -> reaction is scaled by the complex's gate and also by the member gene's own weight, and that member's abundance is already inside the minimum. Every path in this encoder is scaled once per node it crosses, so a complex is no different from any other node on it; exempting complexes from the convention would need an argument that does not exist. Recorded so the choice is visible.

**H2's rewired-expression control does not touch the complex gate, and should.** That control permutes the Human-GEM gene rules and recomputes the reactions' cell-class weights (`rewired_reaction_cell_class_weights`, which returns reaction rows only). A complex takes its gate from `member_of` edges and not from a rule, so the permutation leaves every complex gate intact and the "rewired" condition still carries real expression structure through 1523 complexes. The analogous permutation is of gene-to-complex membership, and extending the control that way is a change to a registered control: it is open decision 4.2b, not a build.
